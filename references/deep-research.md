# Deep research workflow

## Frame the question
Read the [task contract](task-contract.md). Identify entity, exchange where applicable, scope, cutoff, horizon and requested decision. Fetch original reports and [verify](verification.md) comparisons before drawing conclusions.

Lead with an executive takeaway. State the thesis, key supporting evidence, biggest risk and what would change your view. Use the sections relevant to the assignment, not a rigid report template.

## Company analysis
- Business and segments: economic drivers, volume versus pricing, margins, geography, customers, capacity and utilization when disclosed.
- Results: latest quarter and YTD/annual periods, compatible YoY/QoQ comparisons and reported versus adjusted basis.
- Earnings quality: recurring operations versus one-offs, accruals, provisions, disposals, FX, revaluations and discontinued operations.
- Cash: operating cash conversion, working capital, capex, free cash flow with an explicit definition, and financing.
- Balance sheet: debt and cash, net debt definition, maturities, liquidity, covenant exposure when disclosed, dilution and contingent liabilities.
- Outlook: guidance versus external estimates; assumptions and sensitivities. Explain the mechanism rather than extrapolating a headline growth rate.
- Valuation: only with sourced price/date, share count, applicable earnings/debt inputs and a stated method. Compare peers on compatible business/accounting bases. If inputs are unavailable, limit analysis rather than invent a target price.
- Scenarios: base, bull and bear drivers with explicit assumptions and horizons. Numerical cases must be reproducible; probabilities are optional, not default.
- Catalysts: verified events and dates; risks: operational, macro, competitive, financial and regulatory where relevant.
- Counterargument and invalidation: what contrary evidence matters, and which observable developments would disprove the thesis.

## Macro/industry analysis
Identify the series definition, coverage, seasonal adjustment, nominal/real basis, revisions and publication lag. Explain demand/supply, policy transmission and sector exposure. Distinguish leading indicators, nowcasts and official outcomes.

For trade/commodity studies, preserve destination/origin, HS classification, arrivals/departures, wet/dry units, monthly/weekly coverage and sampling limits. Compare estimates with official series only on compatible bases; do not equate them automatically.

## Expectations and market reaction
Use named, dated consensus where accessible. Management targets, a broker's estimate and your forecast are different. A price move is observable; its cause or what is "priced in" is an inference unless supported by evidence.

## Evidence and final delivery
Keep a source/evidence record, original document locations, calculation inputs and formulas. Mark any unsupported section as unavailable or omit it with explanation. Cross-check material disputed claims; avoid double-counting syndicated reporting.

Stop when required questions are adequately supported. Deliver a qualified conclusion if critical inputs remain missing. State analytical uncertainty without burying the main point in disclaimers.
